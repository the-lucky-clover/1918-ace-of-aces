## Recommendation: Rembercourt Aerodrome

The 94th Aero Squadron (the Hat-in-the-Ring squadron) moved through many fields
in 1918. Rembercourt is the correct pick for the in-game home aerodrome:

- Longest home: **1 September – 20 November 1918** (St. Mihiel AND Meuse-Argonne)
- Entire 1st Pursuit Group was there: 27th, 94th, 95th, 147th, and **185th Aero
  Squadrons** — a full pursuit group airfield, not a lonely strip
- SPAD XIII era (the game's aircraft), after the Nieuport 28s were replaced
- Location: 1.6 miles east-northeast of Rembercourt aux Pots, Meuse department,
  northeastern France
- Coordinates: **48°55′08″N 005°12′46″E (48.91889, 5.21278)** — anchor true
  spatial North to this

(Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rembercourt_Aerodrome — coordinates,
units, dates; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/94th_Aero_Squadron — station history)

## Orientation / lighting facts

- **Wind-direction flag**: a period photograph of the 94th flightline at
  Rembercourt (late October 1918) shows a wind-direction flag mounted on the
  headquarters building — it was captured from the German Stenay Airdrome.
  Put that windsock on the in-game HQ building; it's the authentic
  orientation cue and moves with the weather.
- **Solar noon** at ~48.9°N: sun due south, highest arc. **Midnight**: moon.
  The user ordered time-of-day-driven sun angle; these coordinates fix the sky.
- **Night ops are period-real**: the **185th Aero Squadron (Night Pursuit)**
  flew from Rembercourt 7 Oct – 11 Nov 1918. Night sorties from this field
  happened.
- **Dawn/dusk ops**: Lt. Harvey Cook was hand-picked for balloon strafing in
  the early morning and just before dusk; Rickenbacker attacked balloons at
  dusk, lying on the ground. Golden-hour lighting has a historical warrant.
- **Dawn-to-dusk patrols**: during St. Mihiel the squadron flew low-level
  patrols below 600m from dawn until dusk.
- **Weather kills missions**: Epiez, 1 Apr 1918 — rain grounded everything;
  10–11 Nov 1918 — bad weather grounded the squadron on Armistice morning.
  Inclement aerodrome = rain, mud, grounded sorties. Honest.
- **Airfield details (Rembercourt)**: temporary combat airfield; repair depot
  for aircraft AND vehicles (4th Air Park, Flight "C" 648th Aero Squadron);
  various hangars, support buildings, personnel quarters. Rebuilt from French
  Air Service field (1916, reused Aug 1918). After the war: returned to
  agriculture, no trace — today it's cultivated fields south of the D902.
- **Field surface**: grass/dirt. No paved runways — muddy, lived-in, inclement.

## Night vs. noon mood guide (per the user's order)

- **Night launch**: moonlight, flares, dark earth. Night-pursuit reality:
  185th flew nights from this field Oct–Nov 1918. Flare-lit, sparse, tense.
- **High noon launch**: volumetric, ultra-detailed, masterpiece. Full 1st
  Pursuit Group activity — five squadrons' SPADs on the flightline, repair
  depot working, windsock snapping. The war at full volume.
